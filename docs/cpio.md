# Porting libarchive's cpio reader and RPM filter

`hivepack-cpio` reads cpio archives in safe Rust: Linux's initramfs images, the payload of RPM packages, and the archives `cpio`, `afio` and old Unix systems write. It is a port of the cpio reader of [libarchive](https://github.com/libarchive/libarchive), and of its RPM filter, under the BSD-2-Clause license. libarchive's reader takes every cpio format there is, looks for the next header when one is not where it should be, and has been hardened against hostile archives for years.

## The source

The port follows **libarchive 3.8.10**, the latest release as of October 2026. `hivepack_cpio::UPSTREAM_VERSION` names it. As in the other ports ([porting.md](porting.md)), each C file is a module without the `archive_` prefix, functions keep their names in snake case, and each doc comment names its C original.

| libarchive | Port | What |
|---|---|---|
| `archive_read_support_format_cpio.c` | `read_support_format_cpio.rs` (`Cpio`, `Format`, `bid`) | the bid; odc (`070707`), afio's large ASCII headers (`070727`), newc (`070701`), crc (`070702`), binary in both byte orders; the search for the next header; names, symbolic links, `TRAILER!!!`, hard links |
| `archive_read_support_filter_rpm.c` | `read_support_filter_rpm.rs` (`rpm::Reader`, `rpm::bid`) | the bid on a package's lead; the lead, headers and padding taken off |
| `archive_read.c` | `read.rs` (`Input`), `lib.rs` (`Reader`) | looking ahead and consuming in the input; the states of `archive_read_next_header` and `archive_read_data_block`, and the skip of the data not read |
| `archive_entry.c` | `entry.rs` (`Header`, `Device`) | the fields the reader sets, and the link name that symbolic and hard links share |

## What differs

- **Bytes, not C strings.** Paths, link targets and the names in messages are `Vec<u8>`, up to their first NUL, as the archive has them. libarchive converts names only with its `hdrcharset` option, which is not ported (nor `compat-2x`, which only changes the conversion); its warnings for names it cannot convert cannot come up without it. The `pwb` option is here: `Reader::set_pwb`.
- **Devices as the archive has them.** libarchive keeps a device in a `dev_t`: newc's major and minor numbers joined by the system's `makedev`, the other formats' one number as it is, split by `major` and `minor` when asked. [`Device`] keeps what the archive has, `Number` or `Split`, and leaves joining and splitting to whoever makes the device. Hard links are told by the device and inode, as libarchive does; with the numbers kept apart, two devices are one only if their numbers are, as with glibc's `makedev` on Linux. macOS's keeps 8 bits of the major number and 24 of the minor in a 32-bit `dev_t`, so libarchive there can take newc entries on different devices for hard links; the port does not.
- **Modes at full width.** libarchive keeps a mode in a `mode_t`, 16 bits on macOS; the port keeps newc's 32.
- **Errors are a type, with libarchive's messages.** `ARCHIVE_FATAL` is an [`Error`], whose `message` is libarchive's, byte for byte, numbers included. `ARCHIVE_WARN` is the header's `warning`. Where libarchive fails without a message of its own (the archive ends in a header, a name or data, or has no header after garbage), its message is the last one it set since the entry began, which `Error::Unspecified` holds: a "Skipped" warning, or the truncation of the padding after a name, which libarchive notes and goes past. A failure is kept and returned again by every later call.
- **The input is read as libarchive reads an archive in memory** (`archive_read_open_memory`): what the reader looks ahead at is there if the archive has it, however the input comes. The search for a header goes through what is left of the archive in one pass, as libarchive's does in memory; with an input that comes in blocks, libarchive starts the search again at each block, and a header found right where a block starts comes without the "Skipped" warning. The port never seeks: `Reader::new` reads any `io::Read`.
- **Hard links in a map.** libarchive keeps the files with links still to come in a list, which it searches for each entry with more than one link: an archive of many such files takes it time in their square. The list never holds a file twice, so a map gives the same answers.
- **Sizes in 64 bits everywhere.** libarchive checks that a newc name's length and its padding fit in a `size_t`, which fails only where it has 32 bits, with "cpio archive has invalid namelength"; the port computes in 64 bits on every target, and the name is then too long (over 1 MiB) instead.
- **The RPM filter is a reader of its own.** libarchive stacks its filters by their bids, up to 25 of them ("Input requires too many filters for decoding"), the RPM filter among the decompressors. Here `rpm::Reader` gives the payload, and stacking it, under a decompressor or under another package, is the caller's (hivepack-open's).
- **Not ported:** the cpio writers (`archive_write_set_format_cpio_*.c`).

## What libarchive gives that a reader should know

- **The end is `TRAILER!!!`.** An entry of that name ends the archive, and what follows it is not read. An archive without one, cut short, fails where it ends (`Error::Unspecified`, often without a message), after its last entry.
- **Hard links are entries with the same device and inode**, more than one link, and a link still to come: the first one seen is the file, the others are hard links to it (`Header::hardlink`). The data can be on any of them: GNU cpio and libarchive's `bsdcpio` put it on the last in newc and leave the others empty, so an entry can be a hard link and have data, which an extractor writes to the file (libarchive's `archive_write_disk` does). A symbolic link that is also a hard link has the hard link's target as both its links, as libarchive gives it.
- **A symbolic link's target is its data**, which the reader takes: the link then has no data to read, and its size is the target's.
- **crc's checksums are not checked.** libarchive reads `070702` as newc and gives the data as it is, whatever its checksum.
- **Binary archives are not searched.** In the ASCII formats the reader passes over what is not a header, up to the next one ("Skipped N bytes before finding valid header"); in the binary ones, whatever comes after an entry is read as the next header.
- **Not every skip is told.** The warning's message is the last one libarchive set: for an entry after skipped bytes, when the archive ends in the padding after its name, the truncation's (`Warning::Truncated`). And libarchive looks for an odc header with room for afio's larger one, then looks again in the last 115 bytes: a header found right where it looks again comes without the warning. The port gives what libarchive gives.
- **An RPM payload starts at its first byte that is not zero**: the filter takes zeros after the last header for its padding, and fails on a payload of fewer than 16 bytes. Compressed payloads start with their magic, and real ones are longer.

## Bounded resources

As in libarchive: a name and a symbolic link's target are each at most 1 MiB (longer ones are refused), held while the header is read; a file with links still to come keeps its path until its last link, so the archive holds them all at most, as libarchive does. The search for a header looks at 64 KiB at a time, however much garbage there is, and data is read through, never held. An RPM header says its length in 36 bits; it is read through too.

## How it is shown

- **Unit tests** for the numbers (octal and hexadecimal fields that stop at their first other character, the PDP-11's order of 32-bit numbers), the bids, afio's large header, and RPM packages cut short.
- **`tests/oracle.rs`**: comparison with libarchive 3.8.10 through `tools/cpio-oracle`, a C program `scripts/testdata.py` builds from libarchive's sources: its cpio reader, RPM filter, raw format (which gives the filter's output), and cpio writers. Both read every entry and all of its data, or leave it for the next header to skip; the port from an input that comes in pieces of random sizes, its data in pieces of random sizes too. The answers must be the same: the header (format, path, mode, owner, group, links, inode, size, time, the device the file is on and a device file's number, each with its major and minor numbers, symbolic and hard link), its warning, the data's length and FNV-1a hash and how it ends, and how the archive ends; and for the RPM filter, how many filters libarchive stacks and what the last gives, or why the opening fails. The archives:
  - libarchive's 16 cpio and RPM test archives (the `libarchive-cpio` set), with the two parts of its afio test together, its gzipped archive and its packages' payloads uncompressed: read four ways (in memory or as a stream, data read or skipped, with the `pwb` option), and each damaged 150 ways (15 for the largest): a bit flipped or a byte or run of digits changed in a header, a bit anywhere, cut anywhere, bytes put in or taken out, bytes after the end.
  - 300 archives libarchive's writers make from random trees, in odc, newc, binary and PWB: names in ASCII, long, in UTF-8 and with control characters; files, directories, symbolic and hard links (their data on the first or the last), devices, FIFOs and sockets; sizes odd and up to 70 KB; times before 1970 and past 2106. Each read three ways and damaged four times.
  - 1,000 archives the test makes itself (`tests/support/archive.rs`), with what the writers do not make, read twice and each damaged three times: crc with right and wrong checksums, afio's large headers among odc's (negative times and sizes, 64-bit inodes), big-endian binary, upper-case hexadecimal, garbage before headers (up to 70 KB, with pieces of headers in it), hard links in groups whose link counts are right, too many or too few, and links to unrelated files, names without their NUL or with one inside, empty names, symbolic link targets with NULs, empty or of 1 MiB and a byte more, `TRAILER!!!` near-misses and trailers that are links, no trailer, padding with bytes in it or a byte off, sizes and name lengths past the reader's limits and past the archive.
  - 500 RPM packages the test makes, through the filter and read as cpio, and damaged three times: leads of every version and type, no header, one or three, padding or none, headers longer than the package, payloads compressed (by their magic), uncompressed, with zeros first, too short, and packages inside packages, up to 26 deep.

  About 13,000 answers are compared where the oracle is built (Linux and macOS in CI), and every warning and error libarchive's cpio reader and RPM filter can give comes up among them (but the check of a name's length that cannot fail, above); the test checks that the common ones do. `CPIO_SEED=<n> CPIO_COUNT=<count> cargo test --release -p hivepack-cpio --test oracle crafted` compares more archives made by the test: 80,000 more (400,000 answers) were compared on macOS, and as many on Linux (arm64), for the port. On macOS, the answers where `makedev` takes two devices for one (see above, about one in 2,000) are left out. `testdata/oracle/cpio.txt` replays 1,167 answers libarchive gave on Linux everywhere else: libarchive's small archives and damaged copies of them, and archives its writers made, as the requests' bytes, and archives and packages the test makes by number, which it makes again.
- **Fuzzing**: the `cpio` target reads any input, with the `pwb` option or without, every entry and its data, twice, from pieces of the input and in pieces of different sizes: the same headers, data and end both times, the same headers when some data is left to skip, and through the RPM filter when its bid takes the input for a package; no panic. CI seeds it with the archives of the recorded answers, libarchive's small test archives among them.

## Following new releases

A new libarchive release's changes to `archive_read_support_format_cpio.c` and `archive_read_support_filter_rpm.c`, and to what they call in `archive_read.c` and `archive_entry.c`, are ported; the manifest's `libarchive-*-source` set and `UPSTREAM_VERSION` name the release, and the `libarchive-cpio` set takes its new test archives.
