# Opening through layers

`hivepack-open` takes off the layers around what hivepack reads: `backup.tar.zst.age` is a tar archive, compressed with zstd, then encrypted with age, and opens as the tar archive. What is under the layers is told, not read: a 7z archive is told by its signature, kept here, so that telling one does not take `hivepack-7z`. Each layer is found by its first bytes, as `file` and the tools themselves find them, not by the file's name, which is often wrong or missing; and each is read with hivepack's port of its library: `hivepack-age`, `hivepack-zstd`, `hivepack-xz` (xz, lzip, .lzma, and pbzx's chunks), `hivepack-lz4`, `hivepack-deflate` (gzip) and `hivepack-bzip2`; and an RPM package's lead and headers, around its payload, with `hivepack-cpio`'s port of libarchive's RPM filter.

> **Status:** age, zstd, xz, lzip, .lzma, LZ4, gzip, bzip2, pbzx and RPM packages, stacked in any order up to a limit, under which a tar archive, a ZIP archive, a 7z archive, a cpio archive, a XAR archive (a macOS package or `.xip` file), an ISO 9660 image, a UDF volume or plain data; each read as its tools read it, checked with files the reference libraries and age make, and fuzzed. A tar, cpio or XAR archive or an ISO 9660 image under them extracts with `hivepack-extract`: an RPM package's payload, and a macOS package with its payloads expanded. Next: packwing's prompts for passphrases and keys.

## Finding the layers

`detect` looks at the first 1,570 bytes of a stream (`HEAD_LEN`: a tar header block, or an armored age file's 1 KiB of white space and its first line), in this order, and at 96 KiB (`UDF_HEAD_LEN`) for a UDF volume or an ISO 9660 image, whose first bytes are anything (34 KiB, `ISO_HEAD_LEN`, tell ISO 9660 alone):

| What | How it is told |
|---|---|
| age | `age-encryption.org/`, or `-----BEGIN AGE ENCRYPTED FILE-----` after at most 1 KiB of white space, as `age --decrypt` tells armor |
| zstd | the frame magic `28 B5 2F FD`, or a skippable frame's (`50`–`5F 2A 4D 18`), which zstd reads past, followed by anything but LZ4 |
| LZ4 | the frame magic `04 22 4D 18`, the legacy format's `02 21 4C 18`, or skippable frames, which LZ4 shares with zstd, followed by one of them; zstd if the skippable frames go on past what is looked at |
| xz | `FD 37 7A 58 5A 00` |
| lzip | `LZIP` |
| pbzx | `pbzx`: the payload of a macOS package (`.pkg`) or the content of a `.xip` file, as Apple's tools write them |
| gzip | `1F 8B` |
| bzip2 | `BZh` and a block size, `1` to `9` |
| RPM | the lead's magic `ED AB EE DB`, a major version of 3 or 4 and a binary or source package, as libarchive's filter bids on it |
| ZIP | `PK 03 04`, `PK 05 06` (an empty archive), `PK 07 08` (a split archive's marker) |
| 7z | its signature, `37 7A BC AF 27 1C`, as the LZMA SDK checks it (`hivepack_7z::SIGNATURE`) |
| RAR | its signature, `Rar!\x1a\x07` then 0 or 1, or RAR 1.4's `RE~^`, as UnRAR's `IsSignature`. Only told: `hivepack-rar`, under UnRAR's license, is not a dependency of this crate, so it is read through `hivepack_extract::extract_rar` or by itself. One after a self-extracting module is not told |
| XAR | `xar!`, a header of 28 bytes, version 1, and a checksum libarchive knows (none, SHA-1, MD5, SHA-256, SHA-512), as libarchive's bid tells one: macOS packages (`.pkg`), `.xip` files |
| cpio | the magic of odc (`070707`), afio's large ASCII header (`070727`), newc (`070701`) or crc (`070702`), with the whole header in the digits of its format; or the binary format's 16-bit `070707`, in either byte order, with a name of 1 to 1,024 bytes that ends where its length says, as two bytes of magic alone could start any data |
| tar | `ustar` at 257 (USTAR and GNU), or else a checksum that adds up, signed or not, with a name: V7 has no magic |
| UDF | after the same 32 KiB, the volume recognition sequence (ECMA-167 2/8.3): descriptors of 2048 bytes, or of a block when blocks are larger, of type 0 and version 1, among which `BEA01` and then `NSR02` or `NSR03` before `TEA01`, as `file` and Linux look for, in the first 32 descriptors; ISO 9660's come before them in an image with both (Windows' and most DVDs'), which is UDF, its files being on its UDF side |
| ISO 9660 | after its 32 KiB system area (zeros, a boot sector, or a partition table for a USB stick), a volume descriptor: a type (0 to 3, or 255), `CD001` and version 1, as `file` and libarchive look for, without UDF's |
| .lzma | no magic: by the file's name, `.lzma` at its end, once the layers around it are taken off with their extensions (`backup.tar.lzma.age`); under an RPM package, whose payload has no name, by its header, as libarchive's bidder tells one (a properties byte LZMA can have, a dictionary size the LZMA SDK or XZ Utils writes) |

Anything else is data: one file, compressed or encrypted (`notes.txt.zst`), or, with no layer at all, something hivepack does not read. Only a stream whose first 1,570 bytes are data is read further, to 96 KiB, for an ISO 9660 image or a UDF volume: an error of its layer there is given when the data before it has been read, as it would have been without that look. A layer is taken off, and the stream under it looked at again, until a container or data is found, or `Limits::max_layers` (8) are taken off.

## Reading each layer

The decoders are given as `io::Read`, over each library's streaming call, through one adapter. A file reads as its tools read it:

- **Several streams one after the other**, as the tools write and read them: zstd frames, skippable ones among them; gzip members (`cat a.gz b.gz`, and `pigz`); bzip2 streams (`pbzip2` writes one per block); xz streams with their padding; lzip members; LZ4 frames and skippable frames, and the legacy format (`lz4 -l`), which hivepack-lz4's own reader reads as `lz4 -d` does.
- **After the last**: zeros after gzip and bzip2, which tar's blocking leaves when a whole tape image is compressed; and anything after lzip's, as its format allows and `lzip` and `xz` ignore. That is read to its end too, so that the layers around it are read to theirs. Anything else after a stream fails (`trailing data after the end of the stream`), as it does after LZ4's frames in `lz4 -d`. `gzip` and `bzip2` warn about other data and go on; Python's `gzip` fails, as here.
- **pbzx**, which has no description from Apple, is read as Apple's own tools read it, which `pkgutil --expand-full` on macOS 27 showed with packages whose payloads were made by hand: after `pbzx` and a block size (16 MiB in Apple's packages), chunks one after the other to the end of the stream, each with its size and the size of what stores it, both at most the block size. A chunk stored at its own size is stored as it is, even when it starts with xz's magic; any other is one xz stream, with or without a check, that decompresses to exactly the chunk's size. A short chunk can come before others, and a chunk of nothing anywhere (sixteen zeros). Anything else fails, as it does in `pkgutil`: a chunk larger than the block, a stream that decompresses to more or less, two streams in a chunk, a chunk neither stored nor xz, a header cut short, and anything after the last chunk. 7-Zip has no pbzx: it finds the first xz stream, at byte 28, and reads that alone.
- **Cut short**, a stream fails (`the stream is cut short`), as do damaged data, the library's own message given (`incorrect data check`). LZ4's legacy format has no check, and neither do LZ4 frames written without their checksums (`lz4 --no-frame-crc`): damaged data there can read as other data, as in `lz4 -d`.
- **What a library produced before it failed is read first**, and the error at the next read: liblzma, zlib and libbzip2 write what they decoded before an error in the call that returns it, which the adapter keeps, so that what is read does not depend on the size of the reads. libzstd returns an error without what that call decoded, so what is read before its errors can.

An error of a layer's data is an `io::Error` of kind `InvalidData` holding a `DecodeError`, which names the layer and gives the library's message. An error stays: every later read returns it.

## Reading again

`hivepack_extract::extract_tar` reads an archive twice, once to check it all and once to write it, and so opens the file twice, and packwing opens it once more to tell what it is. An `Opener` holds what opens the file (a function, called again each time), its name, the identities that decrypt age layers, and the limits, and each `open` reads the file from its start, through its layers.

The first time an age layer is decrypted, its file key is kept, checked by the header's MAC; each later open decrypts that layer with it (`FileKeyIdentity`, age's `NewInjectedFileKeyIdentity`), so that a passphrase is asked for once, however many times the file is opened, and scrypt runs once: a third of a second at the work factor age encrypts with (2^18), twice that for each step up. Keys for each age layer are kept apart, the outermost first, for age inside age.

A file can change between opens. A later open must find the same layers and container as the first; and an age layer's kept key that does not match its header's MAC means another file encrypted again: both fail with `Error::Changed`. What is under the layers is `hivepack-extract`'s to compare: `extract_tar` compares each header with the first reading's.

`LazyScryptIdentity`, from `cmd/age`, asks for a passphrase only when a layer turns out to be encrypted with one; a file encrypted to keys asks nothing. A file no identity matches fails with `hivepack_age::Error::NoMatch`, whose stanza types (`scrypt`, `X25519`, `ssh-ed25519`) tell what it needs, so that an application can ask for that and open it again.

## Checks at the end of a stream

A compressor checks its data at the end of its stream (gzip's and bzip2's CRC-32, xz's and zstd's checks, lzip's CRC-32, LZ4's content checksum), and age authenticates its last chunk; a tar reader stops at the archive's end, which comes before. `extract_tar` reads on to the end of the stream, in both readings, as GNU tar reads its compressor's output to the end: a stream that fails there fails the first reading, before anything is written. What follows the archive's end is limited to `Limits::max_total_size`, or 1 MiB if that is less, the largest record tar pads to.

## What is left to the caller

- **A ZIP archive under layers** (`archive.zip.age`) is read by random access, from its end: it must be written to a file first, then opened with `hivepack-zip`. A ZIP archive with no layer is opened as it is.
- **A 7z archive** is read by random access too, its headers from where its start header points, near its end: under layers (`backup.7z.zst`), it must be written to a file first. With no layer, `hivepack_7z::Archive::open` opens the file itself, and `hivepack_extract::extract_7z` extracts it.
- **A cpio archive** is read in order by `hivepack-cpio`, under layers too: an RPM package opens as its payload, `Rpm` then the payload's compressor (gzip, bzip2, xz, zstd or LZMA), and `hivepack_extract::extract_cpio` extracts it as it does tar. The package's header, which names the files and their checksums, is taken off, not read: the payload's own headers name the files.
- **A XAR archive** is read in order by `hivepack-xar`, under layers too, and `hivepack_extract::extract_xar` extracts it as it does tar. A macOS package's files are in its payloads, cpio archives under gzip or pbzx named `Payload` (and `Scripts`), and a `.xip` file's in `Content`: `extract_xar` opens each through its layers with an `Opener` over the XAR reader (the payload is its entry's data), and extracts it into a directory of its name, as `pkgutil --expand-full` does.
- **An ISO 9660 image** is read in order by `hivepack-iso`, under layers too (`.iso.xz`, `.iso.gz`), as libarchive reads one without seeking; `hivepack_extract::extract_iso` extracts it as it does tar. With no layer, opening the file itself lets the reader go back for the rare file whose data is before its directory (`hivepack_iso::Reader::seekable`).
- **A UDF volume** is read by random access, as a ZIP archive is: under layers (`.iso.xz` of a Windows image), it must be written to a file first. With no layer, `hivepack_udf::Udf::open` mounts the file itself, and `hivepack_extract::extract_udf` extracts it. Its ISO 9660 side, if it has one, can still be read in order by `hivepack-iso`, under layers too.
- **Data** under layers is one file, whose name is the file's without the layers' extensions; writing it is the application's.

## Bounded resources

The layers are limited (8 by default), and so is what each decoder may allocate: zstd's window to 128 MiB (`zstd_window_log_max`, 27, libzstd's own default; a larger one needs `zstd --long` or `--memory` to decompress too), and liblzma to 1 GiB (`xz_memlimit`; `xz -9` needs 65 MiB). gzip needs its 32 KiB window, bzip2 at most 3.7 MB, and LZ4 a block, 4 MiB at most (8 MB in the legacy format), as their libraries do. Each layer has a 64 KiB buffer. pbzx's chunks are decompressed one after the other, each with its own xz decoder under the same limit. age's limits are its own ([age.md](age.md)): an application that opens files from strangers lowers the scrypt work factor it accepts (`LazyScryptIdentity::set_max_work_factor`; 2^22 by default, 4 GiB and about 5 seconds). What is under the layers, decompressed, is limited by what reads it: `hivepack-extract`'s limits.

## How it is shown

- **`tests/open.rs`**: tar archives compressed by the reference libraries (libzstd, liblzma, liblz4, zlib, libbzip2; LZ4's legacy format from liblz4's blocks; lzip members made with liblzma's raw LZMA1 encoder and lzip's header and trailer, as liblzma has no lzip encoder), each layer alone and four stacked, LZ4 twice among them; several streams one after the other, a skippable frame first and between, zstd's and LZ4's, zeros and xz's padding after; other data after them; each cut short at three places; damaged data, and an outer layer's error coming through LZ4's reader as it is; reads of a byte, into nothing, and from an input that gives a byte at a time and is interrupted between; the limits on layers, zstd's window and liblzma's memory; an error that stays; a file that changes between opens; `.lzma` by its name; and a tar archive under two layers extracted through the opener. A gzip file whose stored data has a byte changed fails `extract_tar` before anything is written: only its CRC-32 tells. pbzx is written by the test, as Apple writes it, with xz streams from liblzma and no check: in one chunk, in chunks of the block size, a short chunk first, stored chunks and chunks of nothing among the others, one stored chunk that starts with xz's magic; and each way `pkgutil` refuses one.
- **`tests/age.rs`**: files age v1.3.2 encrypts, through `tools/age-oracle` ([age.md](age.md)): tar archives under zstd, gzip and xz, a tar archive alone and plain data, to a passphrase or an X25519 key, armored or not, and age inside zstd inside age. Each opens to what opening the plaintext gives, three times, with one prompt for each layer that needs a passphrase; one extracts with one prompt; a wrong passphrase, no matching key (the stanza types say `scrypt`), a prompt refused and a file encrypted again between opens fail with their reasons. Where the oracle is built the files are encrypted afresh; `testdata/oracle/open.txt` holds files age encrypted for everywhere else.
- **XAR**: `testdata/xar`'s archives, made by macOS's `xar`, `pkgbuild` and `productbuild`, open as XAR, and under gzip; a package's `Payload`, opened through an `Opener` over hivepack-xar's reader, is gzip over cpio. A unit test tells XAR's header and near misses (another header size, version or checksum).
- **cpio and RPM**: a newc archive the test writes opens as it is, and under gzip, bzip2, xz, zstd and pbzx; inside an RPM package the test writes (a lead, a signature header padded to 8 bytes, a header), under each compressor rpm uses, LZMA's told by its header, and uncompressed; read through by `hivepack-cpio`, and one, under xz, extracted through the opener with `extract_cpio`. Packages cut in their lead, after it, and in a header, and one with no header, fail in the RPM layer with libarchive's messages, an outer layer's error coming through as it is. libarchive's cpio and RPM test archives, when fetched, open as cpio in each format (binary in both orders, afio's large header, odc, newc) and under gzip, and its packages as `Rpm` and gzip or bzip2. Unit tests tell each cpio header and near misses (a digit out of its format, a name without its NUL, of no length or too long), RPM's lead, and LZMA's header.
- **An ISO 9660 image** of libarchive's tests, kept xz-compressed, opens as one under its layer, and again under gzip and zstd, and alone; `hivepack-iso` reads the same entries and data through the layer as from the image; another, with Rock Ridge and Joliet, extracts through its layer with `extract_iso`, its hard link and symbolic link as files. Data under gzip cut short past the first 1,570 bytes opens, and gives what comes before the error.
- **UDF volumes** of `testdata/udf` ([udf.md](udf.md)), kept xz-compressed: UDF alone with blocks of 512 and 4096 bytes, on a VAT, and genisoimage's beside ISO 9660, each found as one under its layer, under zstd and alone, and mounted by hivepack-udf from what comes out.
- **7z archives** of `testdata/7z`, made by 7-Zip (LZMA2, and AES with the names in the clear): found as one alone and under zstd and xz, and opened by hivepack-7z from what comes out.
- **Unit tests** for `detect`: each magic and near misses, 7z's signature and a byte off, an ISO 9660 image's volume descriptor of each type after a boot sector, and another type, version or identifier, or too short; UDF's volume recognition sequence with descriptors of 2048 and 4096 bytes, after ISO 9660's (and ISO 9660 when only its first 34 KiB are looked at), and NSR before its `BEA01`, after its `TEA01`, of another version or past what is looked at; skippable frames before zstd's and LZ4's and past the head, armor after white space and after too much, ZIP's three signatures, USTAR, GNU and V7 tar with signed and unsigned checksums, and `.lzma` by its name.
- **Fuzzing**: the `open` target opens any input three times with one opener, with the age key of the recorded files and small limits, reading it in pieces of different sizes: each must find the same layers and end the same way, the data the same, or after an error the start of the other's. Its first five minutes found the adapter dropping what liblzma had decoded before an error in the same call, so that what was read depended on the size of the reads; the adapter keeps it now, which `tests/open.rs` checks. The next eight minutes, 865,000 inputs, found nothing, and three more with LZ4, 340,000 inputs.
