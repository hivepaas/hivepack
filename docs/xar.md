# Porting libarchive's XAR reader

`hivepack-xar` reads XAR archives in safe Rust: macOS's installer packages (`.pkg`, flat and product archives), `.xip` files, and what `xar` makes. It is a port of the XAR reader of [libarchive](https://github.com/libarchive/libarchive), under the BSD-2-Clause license, with a port of the parts of [libxml2](https://gitlab.gnome.org/GNOME/libxml2) that reader reads the table of contents with, under the MIT license. libarchive's reader reads the whole table of contents first, then gives the files in the order of their `id`, going to each one's data in the heap; its tests come with the archives that broke it.

A XAR archive is a 28-byte header, a table of contents (TOC) in XML compressed with zlib, and a heap: each file's data and extended attributes where the TOC says, stored or compressed, with checksums of what is stored and of what it decompresses to. The TOC's own checksum is in the heap, where the TOC says.

## The source

The port follows **libarchive 3.8.10**, the latest release as of October 2026, built with **libxml2 2.15.4**, as it is on Linux and macOS. `hivepack_xar::UPSTREAM_VERSION` names libarchive's release. As in the other ports ([porting.md](porting.md)), each C file is a module without the `archive_` prefix, functions keep their names in snake case, and each doc comment names its C original.

| Source | Port | What |
|---|---|---|
| `archive_read_support_format_xar.c` | `read_support_format_xar.rs` (`Xar`) | the header, the TOC's elements and attributes (`xml_start`, `xml_end`, `xml_data`), base64 names, times, numbers, hard links, the heap of files by `id`, extended attributes, decompression (gzip, bzip2, lzma, xz through hivepack-deflate, hivepack-bzip2 and hivepack-xz), checksums (MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-512 through hivepack-crypto) |
| `archive_read.c` | `read.rs` (`Input`), `lib.rs` (`Reader`) | looking ahead, consuming and seeking in the input; the states of `archive_read_next_header` and `archive_read_data_block`, `ARCHIVE_FAILED` and the recovery after it |
| `archive_entry.c` | `entry.rs` (`Header`) | the fields the reader sets: the link name that symbolic and hard links share, the device as a number or its two parts, sizes made 0 when negative |
| `archive_string.c` | `string.rs` | C strings that stop at a NUL; names checked as UTF-8, with CESU-8's surrogate pairs |
| libxml2's `xmlreader.c`, `parser.c`, `parserInternals.c`, `SAX2.c`, `tree.c`, `xmlIO.c`, `uri.c` | `xml/` | `xmlReaderForIO` and `xmlTextReaderRead` as `xml2_read_toc` calls them: the push parser in the pieces the reader pushes, the tree it builds and frees as it goes, the nodes, names, attributes and text it gives, its limits (depth 256, text 10,000,000 bytes, names 50,000), and its warnings and errors at the same places |

libarchive keeps its files and hard links in lists linked through pointers inside the records, the same link serving two lists. The port keeps the records in a `Vec` and links them by index, with the same links, so that a damaged TOC takes the same path through them.

libarchive can read a TOC with expat or Windows' XmlLite instead of libxml2, and they do not give the same (with expat, a name broken by a reference, `a&amp;b`, reads as `a`). The port gives what libxml2 gives.

## What differs

- **Bytes, not C strings.** Paths, names and link targets are `Vec<u8>`, as the TOC has them (decoded from base64 where it says so). libarchive copies them to the locale's charset; the tests run it in a UTF-8 locale, where a name that is not UTF-8 is a warning and no name (`Warning::PathnameConversion` and the like), as here.
- **Data read in part is skipped where it ends.** libarchive's `xar_read_data_skip` adds the last block read, but not yet consumed, to its place in the heap a second time; after an entry's data read only in part (as far as its size, but not to its end), the next entry then seems to be behind it, and libarchive goes back to it, which fails from an input that cannot seek ("Cannot seek"). The port counts the block once: `Reader::new` reads on. libarchive's own extraction reads every entry to its end, and does not meet it.
- **Errors are a type, with libarchive's messages.** `ARCHIVE_FATAL` is an [`Error`], whose `message` is libarchive's, byte for byte; `ARCHIVE_FAILED` is `Error::CannotSeek`, after which the next `next` goes on, as libarchive does; `ARCHIVE_WARN` is the header's `warning`. A failure is kept and returned again by every later call. Where libarchive fails without a message of its own (a number too large for its field), `Error::Unspecified` holds the last message libarchive set since the entry began, which is what `archive_error_string` gives.
- **libxml2's messages are the port's own.** A TOC that is not well-formed fails with "XML Parsing error: " and a description, as in libarchive; after the prefix, the text is not libxml2's. The errors are at the same places: the tests compare every node before them, and which reports are warnings and which errors, with libxml2 itself. After a fatal error, libxml2 goes on to the end of the construct and can report more; the port stops at the first, which is what libxml2 then fails with.
- **Two things libxml2 reads fail.** A document in an encoding other than UTF-8 or US-ASCII (UTF-16, ISO-8859-1, or one iconv knows), and a DOCTYPE with an internal subset (`<!DOCTYPE x [ ... ]>`), fail with an XML error. Every XAR writer writes UTF-8 without a DOCTYPE. A DOCTYPE without an internal subset is read as libxml2 reads it. libxml2's limit on its dictionary of names (100 MB of distinct names) is not ported: the other limits stop a TOC long before.
- **The input is read as libarchive reads an archive in memory** (`archive_read_open_memory`): what the reader looks ahead at is there if the archive has it, however the input comes. `Reader::seekable` goes back, as libarchive does in memory, to data or extended attributes before the place read to; `Reader::new` cannot, and fails that entry with `Error::CannotSeek`, as libarchive does with an input it cannot seek in.
- **Data that is not compressed comes in pieces.** libarchive in memory gives an entry's stored data in one block, all of it, so that a wrong size or checksum at its end fails before any of it is given; the port gives it as it reads it, in pieces no larger than asked for, and fails at the end, as libarchive does from an input that comes in blocks (`archive_read_open_filename`). Compressed data comes in the same 64 KiB blocks from both: the block whose end shows a wrong checksum is not given.
- **"Truncated archive file" where libarchive may not get the memory.** For the TOC's checksum, libarchive first allocates as many bytes as the TOC says it is long; a size larger than the archive has fails with "Unable to allocate copy buffer" where the system refuses the memory, and "Truncated archive file" where it gives it. The port says "Truncated archive file" for every size the archive does not have, without the allocation.
- **Modes and device numbers at full width.** libarchive refuses a mode or device number wider than `mode_t` and `dev_t`, 16 and 32 bits on macOS; the port takes them at Linux's widths, 32 and 64 bits. Device numbers given in parts are put together as glibc's `makedev` does, by the caller (`Dev::Parts`).
- **Times as glibc's `timegm` makes them**, for every date `parse_time` takes (1900 to 9999, with days, hours and seconds past their months' and minutes' ends carried over). libarchive uses the system's `timegm`; the oracle uses glibc's arithmetic on macOS too.
- **Where libarchive never stops, the port does.** A file made a hard link's original twice, when it is the first original of the TOC (`<type link="original">` again, after a nested file made another original), makes libarchive's list of originals a loop, which it goes round for ever after reading the TOC. The port fails as libarchive does for the other repeats: "File with multiple link attributes". The tests check that libarchive does not answer.
- **Not ported:** the writer (`archive_write_set_format_xar.c`); `<signature>` elements, which libarchive does not check either; XmlLite and expat.

## What libarchive gives that a reader should know

- **Entries are in the order of their `id`, not of their paths**, and a directory without anything but a name and a type is not given at all. A path is made of the names of the `<file>` elements it is in, as they were when the inner one was read.
- **A hard link names its original's path**, and every file of the group has the group's number of links; the original need not come first.
- **The device of a device file is `dev`, not `rdev`.** libarchive's reader sets the number the TOC gives (`<deviceno>`, or `<device>`'s major and minor) as the device the file is on, and `rdev` stays 0; extracting a device file needs the former.
- **A wrong extended attribute ends the attributes, not the entry.** An attribute whose checksum is wrong, or that has no name, comes as a warning on the entry, with the attributes before it, and the entry's data is read from where the attribute was left: it is then wrong too, and its checksum says so. Where a name is not UTF-8 and the entry has no extended attributes, libarchive likewise leaves the place where it was, and the data read is what follows.
- **Sizes above `i64::MAX` are 0**, as `archive_entry_set_size` makes them; the data is still checked against the TOC's size.

## Bounded resources

As in libarchive: the TOC is read whole before the first entry, every record kept, and libxml2's limits bound its parts (256 levels of elements, 10,000,000 bytes of text in a node, 50,000 in a name). Its size comes from the archive: a TOC larger than the archive fails. An extended attribute's value is decompressed in one block of at most 64 KiB, and a stored one is as long as the archive has. An entry's data comes in blocks of at most 64 KiB, whatever its size says.

## How it is shown

- **Unit tests** for base64 names, times (February 31, years before 1900 and after 2100, every field out of range), `atou64` at its overflow, `atohex`, the UTF-8 check with CESU-8, and the XML reader on edge cases.
- **`src/xml/tests.rs`**: the XML reader compared with libxml2 2.15.4's xmlTextReader through `tools/xar-oracle`: the nodes `xml2_read_toc` goes through (elements with their attributes, end elements, text, the other node types), what the last read returned, how many bytes the read callback gave, and each warning and error with the number of nodes before it. Edge cases with the read failing at each place, 3,000 random documents (TOC-like or not, damaged), and, with `XAR_XML_LIMITS=1`, documents at libxml2's limits. `XAR_XML_COUNT=<n>` compares more; 100,000 were compared while the port was written.
- **`tests/oracle.rs`**: comparison with libarchive 3.8.10 through `tools/xar-oracle`, a C program `scripts/testdata.py` builds from libarchive's, libxml2's, zlib's, bzip2's, liblzma's and nettle's sources, with, on macOS, glibc's arithmetic for `timegm`. Both read every entry and all of its data, the port in pieces of random sizes; the answers must be the same: the header (path, mode, owner and group with their names, links, size, the times, inode, device and its parts, symbolic and hard link, file flags), its warning, the extended attributes, the data's length and FNV-1a hash (of up to 64 MiB), and how the data and the archive end. Each archive is read in memory and as a stream that cannot seek. The archives:
  - libarchive's test archives: its 9 `.uu` files (`libarchive-xar`, generated from its sources), and the 14 archives `test_read_format_xar.c` holds as C arrays, read from it; each damaged 150 ways: a bit or byte changed anywhere, a header field, cut anywhere, bytes after the end, a byte of the heap, and the TOC's text changed (a number made extreme, a piece of XML put in, a byte, a range deleted or copied, a style, cut short), with its checksum made right again so that the change is read.
  - 300 archives libarchive's writer makes from random trees, read both ways and each damaged four times: stored, gzip, bzip2, lzma and xz, every checksum; names in ASCII, long, in UTF-8 and with what XML escapes; files, directories, symbolic and hard links, devices, FIFOs; extended attributes up to 70 KB, file flags, owners' names and large ids, times before 1970 and after 2100.
  - 7 archives macOS's `xar`, `pkgbuild` and `productbuild` made (`testdata/xar`), read both ways and each damaged 100 times.
  - 500 archives the test makes itself (`tests/support/archive.rs`), read both ways and each damaged three times: TOCs with every element and attribute the reader knows (data and extended attributes in every encoding and checksum, base64 names that are not UTF-8 or hold a NUL, every file type, hard links to originals and to nothing, devices in parts, every BSD and Linux flag, ACLs, unknown elements everywhere, signatures), XML declarations, comments, PIs, CDATA, references, namespaces, elements nested to libxml2's limit, a few hundred files; half of them with numbers too large, ids repeated, checksums, sizes and offsets wrong, data out of order.

  About 8,500 readings are compared where the oracle is built, on Linux and macOS (where libarchive refuses the modes and device numbers its narrower types cannot hold, which the test allows for), and most of libarchive's messages come up among them. `XAR_SEED=<n> XAR_COUNT=<count> cargo test --release -p hivepack-xar --test oracle crafted` compares more. `testdata/oracle/xar.txt` replays libarchive's answers everywhere else, recorded on Linux: libarchive's and macOS's archives and their damaged copies by name and number, archives made by the test by number, which the test makes again, and XML documents for the reader alone.
- **Fuzzing**: the `xar` target reads any input, as an input that can seek and as one that cannot, every entry and its data, twice each, in pieces of different sizes: the same headers, data and end both times, and no panic. CI seeds it with the archives in `testdata/xar`.

## Following new releases

A new libarchive release's changes to `archive_read_support_format_xar.c`, and to what it calls in `archive_read.c`, `archive_entry.c` and `archive_string.c`, are ported; a new libxml2 release's changes to its reader and push parser too, when libarchive's builds take it. The manifest's `libarchive-*-source` and `libxml2-*-source` sets and `UPSTREAM_VERSION` name the releases.
